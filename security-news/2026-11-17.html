8 vulnerabilities added to CISA's exploited-in-the-wild catalog this week.

One week of Security Weekly Digest, 12 stories, as published.

Exploited CVEs

CVE-2026-116606: Barracuda Networks Barracuda Email Security Gateway (ESG)

Barracuda Networks Email Security Gateway (ESG) contains an authentication bypass using an alternate path or channel that could allow an unauthenticated remote attacker to invoke a configuration-import function and restore a crafted, compatible backup archive, resulting in unauthorized modification of the appliance’s global configuration. This vulnerability can be chained with CVE-2026-111560.

Why it matters — Confirmed exploited in the wild. CVSS 9.3 (CRITICAL). CISA remediation deadline 13 Nov 2026 — already passed.

CVE-2026-123039: Check Point Multiple Products

Check Point Firewall, Multi-Domain Security Management Server, Scalable Platforms, Security Gateway, and Security Management Server contain an incorrect permissions assignment for critical resources vulnerability when the Gaia OS DynamicCLI daemon runs as root and listens on a world-writable local socket without authenticating connecting processes. Threat Actors

FBI Warns ARTEX AI Tool Abuse

The FBI issued a Flash notice on November 13, 2026, warning about increasing malicious cyber intrusion campaigns globally that leverage the open-source, AI-powered penetration testing tool ARTEX. Threat actors have used ARTEX since at least August 2026, targeting sectors including financial services, government, and technology, with some intrusions progressing from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours.

Why it matters — Organizations must prioritize defenses against AI-accelerated attacks, as tools like ARTEX significantly compress attack timelines, demanding faster detection and response capabilities.

Vulnerabilities

Microsoft Patches 1,000+ Flaws, Outlook Zero-Day

Microsoft's November 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 1,094 vulnerabilities across Windows, Office, Azure, and other products, including 63 critical flaws. Notably, an actively exploited spoofing vulnerability in Microsoft Outlook (CVE-2026-124194) was patched, which could leak NTLM v2 hashes when a specially crafted file is opened.

Why it matters — Organizations must immediately apply Microsoft's November updates, especially for Outlook, to mitigate the risk of credential theft from an actively exploited zero-day.

Ransomware

AI Fuels Ransomware Surge, Payments Drop

Ransomware attacks increased by nearly 40% in Q3 2026, with threat groups leveraging AI tools to create convincing phishing emails and quickly identify sensitive data. Despite the surge in incidents, fewer organizations are paying ransoms, with the median demand dropping to $700,000 in 2026 from $2 million in 2024, as more companies recover data from backups.

Why it matters — Organizations should prioritize robust backup and recovery strategies over ransom payments, as AI-driven attacks increase volume but reduce attacker leverage against prepared defenses.

Cloud & SaaS

Microsoft 365 Outage Fuels Phishing

A widespread Microsoft 365 outage, disrupting Outlook and other services on November 12-13, 2026, prompted warnings from security agencies about a surge in phishing campaigns. Cybercriminals quickly leveraged the disruption to launch convincing phishing emails and fake support scams, aiming to steal credentials from users desperate to regain access to M365 services.

Why it matters — Organizations must educate users on outage-related phishing tactics and implement robust multi-factor authentication, as attackers exploit service disruptions to compromise credentials.

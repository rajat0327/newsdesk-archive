Policy & Regulation

New IT Rules Mandate 2-Hour Deepfake Takedown

Amendments to the Information Technology (IT) Rules, 2021, notified on February 10, 2026, and effective February 20, 2026, now require social media platforms to remove "sensitive" synthetically generated information (SGI), including deepfakes, within two hours of receiving a complaint. Previously, the deadline was 24 hours. The rules also mandate prominent labeling and embedded metadata for AI-generated content.

Why it matters — Platforms must implement significantly faster content moderation systems and technical measures for AI content, or face legal repercussions for non-compliance.

Sarvam AI Debuts LLM Platform

Bengaluru-based startup Sarvam AI launched Epoch Builder Edition, a developer and enterprise platform designed to help organizations build, fine-tune, and deploy large language models (LLMs) tailored for Indian languages and use cases. The platform features multilingual LLMs (7B and 70B parameters) supporting over 10 Indian languages.

Why it matters — Indian developers and enterprises gain access to a specialized platform and pre-trained models for building AI applications with strong support for local languages, accelerating India-centric AI innovation.

Policy & Regulation

DPDP Act Enforcement Nears, Consent Manager Framework Opens

India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act's 'soft enforcement period' is set to conclude in November 2026, with penalties up to INR 2.5 billion for non-compliance for financial firms. Concurrently, the Consent Manager registration framework is expected to operationalize between June and August 2026, requiring businesses to prepare for new data consent management protocols.

Why it matters — Businesses, especially those handling personal data, must prioritize compliance with the DPDP Act as enforcement tightens and the Consent Manager ecosystem becomes active, necessitating robust data governance and privacy engineering solutions.

Manufacturing

India Expands Semiconductor, Electronics Manufacturing

Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced that three semiconductor plants (Micron, Kaynes, CG Semi) have begun commercial production under Semicon 1.0, with 5-8 more expected in the coming years. The Semicon 2.0 program, approved in July 2026 with a ₹1,27,500 crore outlay, further strengthens India's semiconductor design and manufacturing ecosystem. The PLI scheme for Large Scale Electronic Manufacturing has attracted ₹96,000 crore in investments.

Why it matters — Increased domestic chip and electronics production reduces import dependence and creates opportunities for local suppliers and skilled manufacturing jobs.

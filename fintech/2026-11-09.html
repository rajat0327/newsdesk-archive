Block faces $765m DoJ settlement

Block has reached a preliminary $764 million settlement with the US Department of Justice following investigations into money laundering failures. The settlement addresses systemic lapses in the firm's anti-money laundering (AML) controls.

Why it matters — The $764 million figure establishes a high-water mark for the cost of AML compliance failures in the fintech sector.

Feds Pull Proposals for Reporting Crypto Transactions

The US Treasury’s FinCEN and the Federal Reserve have rescinded proposed 'Travel Rule' and 'Recordkeeping Rule' expansions for cryptocurrency. These rules would have required financial institutions to collect and report detailed data on digital asset transactions.

Why it matters — The withdrawal of these requirements significantly reduces the immediate data-collection and reporting burden for banks handling crypto-to-fiat on-ramps.

India's NPCI creates tech and innovation unit

The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) has established a dedicated technology and innovation unit focused on AI and blockchain. This arm is tasked with evolving the underlying infrastructure of India's national payment systems.

Why it matters — NPCI's move to centralize R&D suggests a shift toward embedding AI and DLT directly into the national payment rail rather than treating them as external layers.

Verifone Deploys Visa’s Featurespace in its Anti-Fraud Framework

Verifone is integrating Visa’s Featurespace technology to analyze over 1,800 transaction attributes in real-time using adaptive machine learning. The system is designed to reduce false declines and chargebacks across its global merchant network.

OnePay Taps Stripe to Power Stablecoin Deposits

Digital bank OnePay is using Stripe-owned Bridge to allow users to fund checking accounts via USDC across 12 different blockchains. The system automatically converts stablecoin deposits into US dollars, bypassing traditional ACH transfer timelines.

Why it matters — This implementation demonstrates stablecoins acting as a functional, multi-chain alternative to the ACH network for retail account funding.

Smarter Security Takes More Than a Bigger AI Model

PayPal’s CISO argues that the value of AI in security depends on the 'harness' around the model rather than the model's raw power. The focus is on building specialized frameworks to manage how cyber-capable models interact with sensitive financial data.

Why it matters — For engineering teams, the takeaway is that enterprise AI safety is a middleware problem, not a model-selection problem.

Regulation

RBI Tightens Non-Bank Cyber Rules

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issued consolidated Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank Payment System Operators (PSOs) on November 5, 2026. These rules mandate stronger board-level oversight, annual system audits, source code protection for critical applications, and stricter third-party security controls. Large and medium PSOs must comply immediately, while smaller operators have until April 1, 2028.

Why it matters — Non-bank PSOs must immediately implement new cybersecurity controls, including source code escrow for critical third-party software and 24/7 manned security operations centers, increasing compliance costs.

Cross-Border

Canada, EU Link Real-Time Payments

Canada and the European Union launched the "Alliance for the Future" on October 30, 2026, which includes plans to connect their respective instant payment systems. Negotiations will commence to link Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) with the Eurosystem's TARGET Instant Payment Settlement (TIPS), aiming to enable seamless cross-border money transfers.

Why it matters — This alliance will enable direct, real-time cross-border payments between Canada and the EU, reducing friction and settlement delays for businesses and consumers.

Fraud & Risk

FCA Explores Quantum for Fraud

The UK's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) established the Quantum Experimentation Group in early November 2026 to test quantum computing's effectiveness in detecting financial crime like money laundering and fraud. The initiative, launching in Q1 2027, invites banks and quantum firms to experiment on a synthetic dataset, with registration open until December 31, 2026.

Why it matters — Financial institutions can participate in regulator-led quantum computing trials to assess its practical application in financial crime detection, shaping future technology adoption and regulatory guidance.

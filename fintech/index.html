Fraud & Risk

Mastercard Seeks Deal Over Card Losses Left by Brazilian Bank Collapse

Mastercard has proposed to pay 50% of the losses claimed by Brazilian merchant acquirers following the collapse of Will Financeira (Will Bank), a fintech linked to the failed Banco Master. The proposal also includes providing multi-year fraud protection services to the affected firms to settle the dispute.

Why it matters — Demonstrates how card networks manage systemic risk and settlement failures by offering partial financial recovery combined with value-added security services to preserve ecosystem trust.

Airwallex Taps Affirm for BNPL

Airwallex has partnered with Affirm to allow merchants in 35 countries to offer BNPL options to US consumers. The integration is designed to work out-of-the-box without requiring merchants to perform additional development work.

Why it matters — Platforms can eliminate integration friction for international merchants by embedding localized payment methods directly into the core payment gateway.

Wall Street’s New Cybersecurity Threat Starts With a Phone Call

Google's Threat Intelligence Group reports that threat actor UNC6671 is targeting financial firms by exploiting cloud-based identity platforms and SaaS ecosystems. Instead of traditional network breaches, attackers are using social engineering to log in directly to collaboration suites.

Why it matters — Attackers are shifting from 'breaking in' to 'logging in' via identity providers, necessitating a move toward identity-centric security over traditional network perimeters.

Wells Fargo to roll out tokenised deposits for corporate clients

Wells Fargo is introducing tokenized deposits for corporate and commercial clients, enabling them to move and settle funds 24/7. The system allows for programmable money movements that bypass traditional banking hours.

Why it matters — Moving from batch-based cycles to 24/7 settlement requires tokenization to bypass the constraints of traditional ledger operating hours.

Consumers Cut Extras as Credit Card Debt Nears a Record

Federal Reserve data shows US revolving credit, primarily credit card balances, grew at a 6% annual rate in June. This occurs as overall consumer credit expanded at 3.3%, indicating a shift toward revolving debt as financial cushions thin.

Why it matters — Rising revolving debt alongside thinning financial cushions suggests consumers are increasingly using credit for essential spending rather than discretionary extras.

Chip Industry Week in Review

SanDisk and SK hynix have released the High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) specification, defining performance expectations, the xPU-HBF host interface, and packaging guidelines for die stacks. Additionally, Samsung demonstrated concept models of zHBM, which utilizes vertical HBM stacking.

Why it matters — Establishes the initial standardization framework for HBF host interfaces and packaging, alongside vertical HBM stacking concepts to address AI memory density bottlenecks.

Nvidia reportedly testing lower memory configs of Rubin Ultra as memory shortage bites back — designs tested include as little as 192 GB and step back to HBM4

Nvidia is reportedly testing lower memory configurations for its Rubin Ultra AI accelerators, scaling designs down to as little as 192 GB. The company is also stepping back to HBM4 in response to ongoing high-bandwidth memory shortages.

Why it matters — Demonstrates how severe HBM supply constraints are forcing leading AI chip designers to compromise on memory capacity and architecture targets.

Chiplet Architectures as a Practical Path to Scalable Automotive Compute

The automotive industry is transitioning from distributed electronic control units (ECUs) to centralized and zonal compute architectures. This shift relies on powerful central processors built using chiplet architectures to deliver scalable, high-performance automotive computing.

Why it matters — Highlights the automotive sector's migration toward chiplet-based central processors as the primary path to scale compute while replacing legacy distributed ECUs.

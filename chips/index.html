Chip Industry Week In Review

GlobalFoundries and TSMC have signed a $2 billion agreement for GF to manufacture silicon interposers at its Malta, New York facility. These components are essential for TSMC’s CoWoS advanced packaging ecosystem, with volume production expected to ramp in early 2028.

GlobalFoundries unveils 7nm-class FDX Fusion node — comes 7 years after firm abandoned FinFET

GlobalFoundries has announced FDX Fusion, a 7nm-class fully depleted silicon-on-insulator (FD-SOI) node to be produced in Dresden. This marks the company's return to advanced scaling seven years after it abandoned FinFET development to focus on specialized low-power platforms.

Why it matters — 7nm FD-SOI offers a power-efficient alternative to FinFET for edge AI and physical AI applications where thermal management is a primary constraint.

Diamond-infused watercooling coldplates for 2,300W Nvidia Rubin drop temps by up to 22C

Diamond-infused watercooling coldplates have been developed to manage the 2,300W thermal load of Nvidia’s upcoming Rubin GPUs. Initial testing indicates these advanced thermal interfaces can reduce hotspot temperatures by up to 22°C compared to traditional copper designs.

Why it matters — As AI accelerator power consumption exceeds 2kW, the thermal conductivity of copper is becoming a bottleneck, necessitating a shift to diamond-based cooling solutions.

Startup Funding: Q3 2026

Q3 2026 funding data reveals a shift toward AI inference startups that utilize on-chip SRAM or direct-stacked commodity DRAM to bypass HBM supply constraints. Two such companies secured $1 billion each, reflecting investor interest in architectures that solve the memory wall without expensive HBM.

Why it matters — The high cost and limited capacity of HBM are forcing a move toward architectures that keep models in on-chip SRAM or use 3D-stacking with standard DRAM.

Applied Materials Bets $5B on a New Model for Chip Innovation

Applied Materials is committing $5 billion to a new R&D model designed to accelerate the development of next-generation transistors and interconnects. The initiative aims to shorten the innovation cycle for AI-driven silicon by co-developing equipment and processes with major chipmakers.

Why it matters — The $5B investment reflects a shift where the speed of equipment R&D is becoming as critical as the technology itself to meet AI-driven demand.

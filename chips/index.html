Vertical DRAM Stacking Expands Thermal Design Space Beyond HBM-on-GPU (imec)

Researchers at imec evaluated a 3D memory architecture that vertically stacks DRAM dies directly above a GPU, incorporating cooling cavities between layers. This approach serves as a thermal alternative to traditional 2.5D HBM and direct-stacked HBM-on-GPU, using simulations to define the thermal design envelope for various stack heights.

Why it matters — Rotating DRAM dies vertically creates physical space for cooling cavities, potentially bypassing the thermal density limits that currently constrain 2.5D and standard 3D HBM integration.

Self-Heating And Parasitics Challenge Multi-Tier CFET Scaling (TU Munich, IIT Kanpur)

A study by TU Munich and IIT Kanpur analyzes the impact of self-heating effects (SHEs) and parasitics in multi-tier Complementary Field-Effect Transistor (CFET) designs. The research specifically examines how vertically stacking multiple nanosheet devices introduces middle-of-line (MOL) and back-end-of-line (BEOL) induced parasitic challenges.

Why it matters — As scaling moves toward vertical CFET architectures, the thermal resistance of the stack and parasitic capacitance in the MOL become the primary bottlenecks for performance rather than just gate dimensions.

The AI Packaging Challenge: 5 Reasons Panels Are Gaining Momentum

Lam Research is advocating for panel-level processing as a solution to AI chip packages exceeding traditional wafer size limits. The shift involves moving from circular silicon wafers to large-format rectangular panels to scale advanced packaging for the next decade of high-performance compute.

No New Name Has Replaced “UX”

A survey of 604 design and tech professionals reveals that 'UX' remains the dominant term for the field despite industry layoffs and budget cuts. While practitioners frequently debate the industry's identity, alternative naming conventions remain highly fragmented.

Why it matters — Despite internal industry anxieties and rebranding attempts, 'UX' remains the only universally recognized term for the discipline among external stakeholders.

Dogfooding vs. QA vs. User Research

While dogfooding helps teams identify technical bugs, it cannot substitute for genuine user research. Internal employees possess too much product knowledge and context to accurately represent real-world users.

Why it matters — Internal employees suffer from a curse of knowledge that prevents them from experiencing usability issues the way actual customers do.

Design Systems

Defining ethical design for machines

As AI systems increasingly generate and evaluate interfaces, design standards must evolve beyond human-readable Figma components. Ethical design principles need to be translated into structured, machine-readable rules that algorithms can interpret and follow.

Why it matters — Design systems must transition from visual component libraries into machine-readable rule engines to ensure AI co-designers respect ethical and brand boundaries.

UX Collective · 09 August 2026 · Read the original →

Tooling

What your AI co-designer can’t infer from your hex values

To maximize the quality of AI-generated design work, designers should establish dedicated context files when onboarding LLMs like Claude. These files provide the deep project background and constraints that raw design assets like color codes cannot convey.

Why it matters — Supplying structured context files to LLMs at the start of a project dramatically improves the relevance and quality of AI-assisted design outputs.

Visual

Why Your Favorite Websites Look Like 2005 (And Why You Secretly Love It)

Major platforms like Google and Amazon maintain dense, older-looking interfaces because they prioritize speed and user trust over modern aesthetics. While boutique brands chase minimalist trends, these giants leverage familiar, high-density layouts to optimize conversion and usability.

Why it matters — High-density, familiar, and 'ugly' layouts often outperform modern, minimalist aesthetics by reducing cognitive load and maximizing user trust.

Interaction

The 2-Second Rule: How to make your website feel like magic in 2026

Modern web development is moving away from traditional loading indicators by leveraging predictive AI and Edge architecture. These technologies anticipate user actions to load content before it is requested, making interactions feel instantaneous.

Why it matters — Utilizing predictive loading and Edge computing can eliminate perceived latency entirely, shifting the UX paradigm from reactive loading to proactive rendering.

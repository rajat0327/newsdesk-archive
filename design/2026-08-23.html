AI-Generated Images Can Perform as Well as Stock Photography

A study testing user perceptions of AI-generated website imagery compared to traditional stock photography. The research found that when users are unaware of the image's origin, AI-generated images incur no trust or quality penalty, offering a low-cost alternative for design teams.

Why it matters — AI-generated imagery can be used interchangeably with stock photos without degrading user trust, provided the images maintain a polished, professional aesthetic.

Building Tactile UX: Honoring Intentional Design With Lottie

Explores the implementation of tactile, bouncy, or destructive web interfaces using Lottie animations. It contrasts this approach with the industry default of using heavy, complex physics engines like Matter.js or Cannon.js.

Why it matters — Using Lottie for tactile UI animations provides a lightweight, highly controllable alternative to resource-heavy physics engines.

AI & Design

AI is lying to us, and nobody seems to care

Examines the UI challenges of designing interfaces for LLMs that confidently present fabricated information. It discusses the design trade-offs of introducing UI elements, like side panels, to expose the system's uncertainty and source-tracking to users.

Why it matters — Designers must build interface patterns that actively communicate uncertainty and system confidence rather than relying on a single, deceptively polished output.

Figma is the New Dreamweaver: How Modern Prototyping Tools are Trapping Us in 2018

Critiques modern prototyping tools like Figma for creating a "perfect canvas" illusion that distances designers from the realities of web code. It argues that relying too heavily on pixel-perfect static designs obscures critical system behaviors, performance constraints, and responsive layouts.

Why it matters — Over-reliance on visual canvas tools can isolate design from engineering; teams must prioritize understanding code-based systems, behavior, and performance over static pixels.

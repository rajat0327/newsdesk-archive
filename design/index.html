Context Libraries: How AI Power Users Manage Context Engineering

A Nielsen Norman study of AI power users reveals that while agents can handle execution, users struggle with the high cognitive load of curating, locating, and updating context files. The research suggests that context engineering is becoming a primary bottleneck for expert users of tools like Claude.

Why it matters — Context curation is becoming a primary user burden in AI-driven interfaces, shifting the user's role from 'creator' to 'librarian'.

Bounded delegation: How much authority should AI have?

This post introduces a model for 'bounded delegation' to help product teams decide which decisions AI can make autonomously and which require human oversight. It uses the example of AI-generated labels for customer feedback to show how unverified AI assumptions can lead to flawed product experiments.

Why it matters — Use a bounded delegation model to prevent AI from making unverified assumptions about business logic or user intent without human checkpoints.

Synthetic users are making me rethink “imperfect research”

The author argues that synthetic users introduce a unique risk of 'false confidence' that traditional 'imperfect' research does not. Unlike small-sample human studies where uncertainty is visible, AI-generated responses can appear authoritative while being entirely disconnected from real human behavior.

Why it matters — Synthetic data creates a risk of false confidence that is more dangerous to a product's direction than the known uncertainty of small-sample human research.

UX Collective · 11 October 2026 · Read the original →

The New Siri’s Biggest Strength Is Not Intelligence

An analysis of Apple's AI strategy suggests that the utility of an assistant depends more on its access to personal context—messages, photos, and schedules—than on the raw intelligence of the LLM. The focus is on the interface's ability to help users understand the limits of that context.

Why it matters — Utility in AI assistants is driven more by access to local, personal context than by the underlying model's raw intelligence.

Web Designers Love Dark Mode Toggles. Users Might Not Care.

This piece questions the ubiquity of manual dark mode toggles in web design, suggesting they may be a designer-led trend rather than a user necessity. It argues for relying on system-level preferences rather than forcing users to manage a dedicated UI element for brightness.

Why it matters — Question whether a manual toggle provides actual user value or simply adds interface clutter that could be handled by system-level preferences.

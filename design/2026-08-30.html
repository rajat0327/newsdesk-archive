Design Systems

Stop using OKLCH lightness for your color scale

While the OKLCH color space is highly effective for generating colors, its lightness value does not accurately predict perceived contrast. Relying solely on OKLCH lightness to define color palette weights can result in accessibility and contrast issues.

Why it matters — Do not rely on OKLCH lightness values to guarantee accessible contrast ratios; design systems require a different metric or manual verification to ensure proper palette weights.

AI & Design

The Custodial Era of UX: Cleaning Up After AI

The rapid speed of AI-assisted development, prototyping, and 'vibe coding' is outpacing the speed at which UX teams can evaluate designs, leading to massive UX debt. To adapt, UX professionals must shift from slow, manual evaluations to building shared design judgment and accelerating their feedback loops.

Why it matters — When development velocity increases through AI generation, UX teams must pivot from gatekeeping to establishing systemic guardrails and accelerated evaluation methods to prevent rapid accumulation of UX debt.

Research

AI Can't Replace Real Research in Empathy Mapping

While generative AI tools can synthesize and organize existing user data, they cannot manufacture authentic user insights or simulate the messy, real-world experiences of actual humans. Relying on AI-generated personas or simulated user feedback to populate empathy maps risks creating workshops detached from real user evidence.

Why it matters — AI should only be used to organize and structure empirical research data that has already been collected, rather than being used to simulate or generate user behaviors and frustrations.

Interaction

Nobody wants a blank page. Nobody touches a perfect one.

Users experience psychological friction at both extremes of the design spectrum: they struggle to initiate action on a completely blank canvas, yet they are hesitant to edit or modify a state that appears polished and finished. Effective interaction design must provide scaffolding to help users start, while keeping editable states approachable.

Why it matters — To encourage user contribution, interfaces must avoid both the intimidation of a blank canvas and the psychological barrier of a seemingly 'perfect,' unalterable finished state.

The Infinite Scroll Was a Design Crime. Now Meta is Paying for It.

The infinite scroll pattern, originally designed to improve usability, has evolved into an addictive mechanism designed to maximize user retention at the cost of human willpower. As major tech companies face legal scrutiny over algorithmic addiction, designers must re-evaluate the ethical implications of patterns that eliminate natural friction.

Rethinking Data Visualisation: A UX Approach To Dashboards That Actually Drives Decisions

Despite the widespread availability of data and dashboard tools, many corporate dashboards fail to drive actual decisions because they simply present numbers without context. A UX-driven approach to data visualization focuses on structuring information to prompt specific actions and decisions during reviews rather than just displaying data.

Why it matters — Dashboards must be designed around the specific decisions they need to facilitate, rather than simply aggregating and displaying available data points.

Design Systems

Stop Treating CSS Container Queries Like Traditional Media Queries

This piece explores the fundamental shift from viewport-based media queries to component-based container queries in CSS. It addresses the common misconception that they are interchangeable and explains the architectural benefits of letting components respond to their own dimensions.

Why it matters — Container queries enable a "design once, deploy anywhere" approach for design system components, decoupling them from the global page layout.

The 3 Roles of Context for AI Agents

Research into AI-agent power users reveals three distinct layers of context required for high-quality outputs: global, local, and ambient. The study explains how the lack of these specific context types leads to generic or problematic results in complex tasks.

Why it matters — Designing for AI agents requires building interfaces that allow users to curate and feed these three specific types of context into the system.

Chrome Can Now Measure Exactly How Obnoxious Your Website’s Ads Are

Google Chrome has introduced tools to quantify ad intrusiveness using specific metrics like screen real estate coverage, data usage, and CPU consumption. These public measurements allow designers and engineers to audit the actual user cost of third-party scripts and overlays.

Why it matters — It provides an objective, data-driven standard for measuring "obnoxious" UX, moving the conversation from subjective taste to measurable performance impact.

Why it matters — It provides a framework for maintaining product coherence when integrating autonomous features that would otherwise lead to "feature soup."

Meta Just Launched an AI That Uses Websites for You. That Could Be a Big Deal for Web Designers

Meta’s Muse agent is an AI capable of navigating websites and completing forms autonomously, signaling a shift in who—or what—is using the web. This development suggests that web design must now account for non-human agents that prioritize structure and clarity over visual flair.

Why it matters — It forces a transition toward "agent-accessible" design, where the underlying semantic structure of a site is as important as its visual interface.

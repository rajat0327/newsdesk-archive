Can AI Generate Good Designs from Raw User Data?

Evaluates whether generative AI design tools perform better when provided with raw user comments or synthesized research findings and recommendations. The study shows that AI produces significantly better UI concepts when supplied with structured synthesis rather than unanalyzed user feedback.

Why it matters — AI design generators require synthesized research insights and recommendations rather than raw transcripts to produce viable UI layouts.

10 Lessons Learned from Auditing 100 Online Stores

Audits 100 major e-commerce sites to measure the prevalence of dark patterns such as fake urgency timers and hidden checkout fees. The findings show that up to 68% of audited stores use deceptive mechanisms, which directly correlate with a 70% cart abandonment rate.

Why it matters — Deceptive patterns like fake countdown timers drive cart abandonment up to 70% while eroding long-term brand trust.

UI edge cases: A reference guide

Provides a structured reference guide for identifying, testing, and fixing UI edge cases prior to shipping. It breaks down edge cases by category and outlines detection strategies alongside concrete design fixes.

Why it matters — Categorizes non-standard state failures to establish systematic pre-release testing and remediation workflows.

Send the Firefighters to the Fires

Explores exception-first UI design patterns in enterprise software, drawing from industrial control systems and early computing models. It details why interface views should prioritize displaying system failures and actionable errors over static metrics.

Why it matters — Enterprise operational interfaces should default to displaying exceptions and system errors rather than healthy status metrics.

Qualitative User-Research Methods: An Overview

Breaks down four core qualitative user research methods: usability testing, user interviews, field studies, and diary studies. It maps each technique to specific research questions regarding user needs and friction points.

Why it matters — Match qualitative methods to your objective—use field studies for environmental context, diary studies for longitudinal behavior, and usability tests for direct execution friction.

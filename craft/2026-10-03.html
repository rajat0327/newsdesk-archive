Article: High Availability Is Not Resilience: Why Cloud Systems Fail When It Matters Most

A routine TLS 1.3 upgrade silently broke Route 53 health checks, causing a CDN to stop routing traffic to a healthy region while internal dashboards showed no issues. The post analyzes the critical differences between high availability and resilience, highlighting how control-plane dependencies create invisible failure modes.

Uber Eats Rebuilds Search Pipeline to Cut End-to-End Latency by 50%

Uber rebuilt its Uber Eats search pipeline to achieve a 50% reduction in end-to-end latency. The overhaul involved implementing Above-the-Fold measurement, reducing retrieval work, parallelizing hydration, and redesigning advertising data.

Why it matters — A 50% latency reduction can be achieved at scale by parallelizing hydration and optimizing retrieval work rather than relying solely on infrastructure upgrades.

Bliki: Sensible Default

Martin Fowler introduces the concept of 'Sensible Defaults' as practices that should be used absent overriding context, contrasting them with the often-disliked term 'best practices.' Examples include version control, separating UI from domain logic, and automated deployment pipelines.

Why it matters — Framing engineering standards as 'sensible defaults' rather than 'best practices' acknowledges context-dependency and reduces developer resistance while still establishing clear baseline expectations.

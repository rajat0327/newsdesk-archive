Multimodal

Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding

This research deconstructs multimodal vision encoding into three distinct design variables: visual priors from pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and the placement of that capacity relative to the language model fusion. It evaluates the trade-offs between using pretrained encoders, training from scratch, or removing encoders entirely.

Why it matters — Decoupling visual priors from model capacity allows for more modular VLM design and identifies exactly where vision-specific compute provides the most benefit.

Multimodal

V-JEPA-LM: Video-Language Models That Read Dynamics Beyond Static, Text-Aligned Representations

The paper investigates why video-language models often fail to grasp temporal dynamics, finding that text-alignment training encourages models to retrieve specific attributes rather than 'read' the scene. It explores joining world-model encoders like V-JEPA, which learn scene changes from raw video, with language models to preserve dynamic information.

Why it matters — It identifies a 'retrieval vs. reading' failure mode where text-aligned training can actually suppress a model's ability to understand complex physical dynamics.

arXiv · 18 November 2026 · Read the original →

Systems & Inference

MMFlash: Faster Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language Models

MMFlash adapts speculative decoding for vision-language models by using diffusion-based drafters to propose token blocks. It introduces a novel metric for token-level visual dependence to optimize when the drafter needs to account for visual context versus relying on text history.

Why it matters — The visual dependence metric allows speculative decoders to adaptively balance drafting speed with the computational cost of processing visual context.

arXiv · 18 November 2026 · Read the original →

Multimodal

JVT: Just Video Transformer makes pixel-space video diffusion simple

JVT (Just Video Transformer) demonstrates a simplified approach to pixel-space video diffusion using a large-patch DiT-style Transformer. It eliminates common complexities such as pretrained autoencoders, auxiliary detailers, cascaded stages, and U-Net skip connections.

Why it matters — Direct pixel-space modeling with large-patch Transformers can match the performance of complex cascaded diffusion pipelines while significantly simplifying the training and inference architecture.

arXiv · 18 November 2026 · Read the original →

Multimodal

ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters

This study scales Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to 552 billion parameters to test if frontier-scale representations improve physical robot control. It examines whether the broad knowledge gained from massive VLM pretraining translates into better performance on embodied tasks compared to smaller, specialized policies.

Why it matters — It establishes the scaling laws for embodied AI, determining if model size is a primary bottleneck for translating visual understanding into physical action.

arXiv · 18 November 2026 · Read the original →

Evaluation & Benchmarks

SWE-CoWork: Benchmarking Coding Agents as Digital Employees in Organizations

SWE-CoWork benchmarks coding agents by placing them in a simulated organization with 26 virtual colleagues across 101 real repositories. Unlike standard coding tasks, agents must collaborate, establish requirements, and verify deliveries within a stateful, multi-actor workflow.

Why it matters — Shifting evaluation from isolated code generation to organizational collaboration reveals how agents handle the social and procedural complexities of real-world software engineering.

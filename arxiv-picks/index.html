Eat Your Precision: Precision Objective Mitigates Reward Hacking in Rubric-based RL

Rubric-based rewards used during reinforcement learning on non-verifiable tasks primarily reward rubric coverage, turning RL into a recall-maximizing objective. Consequently, models suffer from reward hacking by producing overly verbose responses filled with unnecessary or incorrect facts. Incorporating an explicit precision objective alongside recall mitigates this failure mode.

Why it matters — Rubric-based RL rewards act as recall-only objectives that induce verbosity and hallucinations, which can be counteracted by adding a precision penalty.

SANA-Video 2.0: Hybrid Linear Attention with Attention Residuals for Efficient Video Generation

SANA-Video 2.0 is introduced as a hybrid video diffusion transformer, scaled to 5B and 14B parameters, designed for high-quality 720p video generation on a single GPU. It achieves quality comparable to full-softmax video DiTs while maintaining the favorable long-sequence scaling of linear attention by employing Hybrid Linear-Softmax Attention and Attention Residuals.

Why it matters — This paper details architectural innovations, specifically hybrid attention and attention residuals, that enable efficient and high-quality video generation, addressing a key scaling challenge in multimodal models.

Trajectory2Gym: Decision-Grounded Compositional Interaction Synthesis for Tool-Use Agents

Successful execution logs do not automatically translate into effective training trajectories for tool-use agents. Successful interactions frequently include actions unjustified by the agent's visible observation state or fail to capture multi-step dependencies. Trajectory2Gym formalizes requirements for trajectory supervision, emphasizing compositional coverage to ensure training data grounds decisions in visible information.

Why it matters — Training tool-use agents on raw successful trajectories fails when actions rely on hidden context or omit structural dependencies, requiring decision-grounded state filtering.

Memory Tax: Dissecting the Net Value of Agent Memory

This empirical study evaluates whether building dedicated structured memory systems for long-horizon LLM agents delivers positive net value over simple retrieval. Across multiple benchmarks and model backbones, many complex agent memory architectures perform worse than naive RAG operating over raw interaction history. The authors dissect the trade-offs of memory frameworks to isolate where state tracking adds noise versus actual utility.

Why it matters — Complex structured memory modules often underperform simple RAG over raw history, showing that context abstractions can add negative net value unless carefully benchmarked.

Rollback: Experience Backtracking in World Models for Policy Improvement

Action-conditioned world models are typically deployed as forward-only physical surrogates for robot policy learning. The Rollback framework introduces experience backtracking, enabling policies to rewind simulation state to historical decision points to sample counterfactual futures. This technique exploits a simulator-native capability that physical environments cannot offer.

Why it matters — Unlocks counterfactual policy exploration in action-conditioned world models by rewinding execution to past decision branch points instead of running forward-only rollouts.

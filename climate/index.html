Georgia PSC Approves Secret OpenAI Contract

The Georgia Public Service Commission staff approved a contract between Georgia Power and OpenAI to power a planned 3.2-gigawatt hyperscale data center in Effingham County. The approval bypassed a direct vote from the commission itself, drawing criticism from environmental groups over the secretive process.

Why it matters — Illustrates the massive scale of individual AI data center power demands (3.2 GW) and the regulatory shortcuts utilities are taking to secure these contracts.

Industry & Heat

Hydrogen Is Still A Tonnes Market, No Matter How Many MWh We Assign To It

Global hydrogen demand surpassed 100 million tonnes in 2025, with almost all consumption concentrated in traditional industrial applications like refining and ammonia production. The post argues that treating hydrogen primarily as an energy storage medium (measured in MWh) ignores its actual role as a physical chemical feedstock.

Why it matters — Decarbonizing hydrogen must focus on replacing fossil-based feedstocks in existing chemical and refining processes rather than chasing speculative power-to-gas energy storage markets.

Could we use Trump's tricks to make good green policy?

This post discusses a collection of 17 essays from the Roosevelt Institute detailing how a future administration could use aggressive economic statecraft for decarbonization. It focuses on tools like taking federal equity stakes in private companies, utilizing new federal authorities, and establishing sovereign wealth funds.

What should state policymakers do about data centers?

This episode analyzes the rapid growth of data center electricity demand, noting that 15 states accounted for 80% of US demand in 2023. A single hyperscale campus can consume 15% to 20% of a utility's capacity, while building the necessary transmission takes 5 to 15 years.

Why it matters — Highlights the severe operational mismatch between the rapid deployment of hyperscale computing facilities and the 5-to-15-year timeline required to build supporting transmission infrastructure.

Climate Change Is Rewriting Europe’s Nuclear Cooling Assumptions

Romanian engineers used 180 kg of explosives to clear a rock outcrop in the Danube's Bala Canal. The blast allowed the construction of a temporary dam to maintain cooling water levels for a nuclear plant during low river flows caused by extreme heat.

Why it matters — Highlights how climate-induced low river levels force extreme, invasive civil engineering interventions to prevent thermal power plant shutdowns.

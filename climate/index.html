He helped Uruguay decarbonize. Can he help other countries do the same?

Ramón Méndez Galain explains how Uruguay transitioned to a 98% fossil-free grid by utilizing its existing reservoir hydropower assets as a 'battery' to balance variable wind and solar. The transition relied on a redesigned market structure and cross-party political consensus to attract private capital while keeping the utility public.

Why it matters — Provides a proven playbook for integrating high penetrations of variable renewables by using existing reservoir hydropower as a dispatchable battery.

2 GW HVDC Is Becoming Standard. The Rest Of The Grid Isn’t.

High-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission is transitioning from a niche solution for subsea or long-distance lines to a standardized 2 GW grid integration technology. However, integrating these massive point-to-point direct current links into legacy alternating current (AC) grids presents significant technical challenges.

Why it matters — Highlights the engineering shift toward standardizing 2 GW HVDC systems and the resulting integration challenges with legacy AC grid infrastructure.

Grid & Transmission

PJM Eyes Surplus Interconnection Pathway

PJM Interconnection is considering rule changes to create a pathway for adding generation and battery storage near existing power plants using surplus interconnection capacity. An earlier reform effort in early 2025 yielded limited results, with only two of eight applications approved since 2023. MISO and SPP have seen more success, with 44 and 22 projects, mostly battery storage, coming online in about one to two years respectively.

Why it matters — PJM's renewed focus on surplus interconnection aims to accelerate grid integration for new generation and storage, potentially reducing costs and timelines for developers.

What should state policymakers do about data centers?

This episode analyzes the rapid growth of data center electricity demand, noting that 15 states accounted for 80% of US demand in 2023. A single hyperscale campus can consume 15% to 20% of a utility's capacity, while building the necessary transmission takes 5 to 15 years.

Compressed-Gas Storage Demonstrators Got Bigger. The Economics Didn’t Get Better.

This post examines the economics of compressed-gas electricity storage in light of recent large-scale projects. It highlights China's commissioning of the 600 MW / 2.4 GWh Huai'an compressed-air demonstration and Hydrostor's ongoing efforts. The author argues that despite these larger demonstrators, the fundamental economics of the technology have not improved.

Why it matters — Scaling up physical capacity to gigawatt-hour levels does not inherently solve the unfavorable round-trip efficiency and high capital costs of compressed-gas storage.

Major US offshore wind farm installs its last turbine

The $6.2 billion Revolution Wind offshore wind farm has successfully installed its 65th and final turbine. The project reached this milestone despite facing two separate stop-work orders from the Trump administration. The wind farm is already actively producing carbon-free power for the U.S. grid.

Why it matters — Large-scale offshore wind projects can successfully reach completion and deliver power despite facing multiple federal regulatory stop-work orders.

Analysis: Global fossil-fuel emissions set to fall in 2026 amid Hormuz crisis

Global fossil-fuel emissions are projected to fall by 0.5% in 2026 due to trade disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz. The ongoing conflict has spiked global oil and gas prices, suppressing consumption across the global economy.

Why it matters — Geopolitical supply shocks and resulting price spikes can drive immediate, macro-level reductions in global fossil fuel consumption.

What should state policymakers do about data centers?

This episode analyzes the rapid growth of data center electricity demand, noting that 15 states accounted for 80% of US demand in 2023. A single hyperscale campus can consume 15% to 20% of a utility's capacity, while building the necessary transmission takes 5 to 15 years.

Why it matters — Highlights the severe operational mismatch between the rapid deployment of hyperscale computing facilities and the 5-to-15-year timeline required to build supporting transmission infrastructure.

Climate Change Is Rewriting Europe’s Nuclear Cooling Assumptions

Romanian engineers used 180 kg of explosives to clear a rock outcrop in the Danube's Bala Canal. The blast allowed the construction of a temporary dam to maintain cooling water levels for a nuclear plant during low river flows caused by extreme heat.

Why it matters — Highlights how climate-induced low river levels force extreme, invasive civil engineering interventions to prevent thermal power plant shutdowns.

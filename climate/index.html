ANALYSIS: Solar & Storage Growth Drives Down Summer Wholesale Electricity Prices by 42% in Texas & 51% in California

An analysis from the Solar Energy Industries Association shows that solar and battery storage deployment drove down summer wholesale electricity prices by 42% in ERCOT (Texas) and 51% in CAISO (California). The figures demonstrate how renewables and storage mitigate wholesale power spikes during peak summer demand.

Why it matters — Grid-scale solar combined with battery storage dampens peak wholesale power prices during extreme summer heat waves across distinct ISO market structures.

Leading small modular nuclear reactor will be late and over budget

Ontario Power Generation announced that North America's first small modular reactor project, GE Hitachi's BWRX-300 at the Darlington site, will miss its target completion date of October 2030 and exceed its initial budget. The delay underlines persistent schedule and cost challenges facing first-of-a-kind SMR deployments.

Why it matters — First-of-a-kind small modular reactors remain subject to the same cost escalation and construction delays that historically impacted large-scale nuclear builds.

Another novel nuclear project gets green light to start construction

The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission approved a joint application by X-energy and Dow Chemical to construct four Xe-100 high-temperature gas-cooled advanced reactors at a Texas industrial facility. The plant is designed to generate both high-grade process steam and power directly for industrial operations.

Why it matters — Co-locating high-temperature gas reactors at industrial facilities provides a regulatory pathway to decarbonize heavy industrial process heat alongside electricity.

Four of Seven Dutch Green Hydrogen Projects Have Been Cancelled. The Subsidies Keep Coming.

Out of seven major green hydrogen electrolysis projects awarded €783.5 million in public subsidies by the Netherlands in 2022, four have been cancelled and none are operational today. Despite the lack of active production, the government is preparing a subsequent funding round.

Why it matters — Capital subsidies for upstream hydrogen production fail without verified off-take commitments from industrial end users.

The federal permitting bill has been updated. What changed?

A 77-page manager's amendment to the U.S. Senate's bipartisan permitting bill incorporates changes to transmission planning, environmental review deadlines, and tribal protections. It also introduces provisions requiring large electricity consumers, such as data centers, to directly fund dedicated grid upgrades.

Why it matters — Permitting reform is shifting grid expansion costs directly onto large off-grid loads like data centers rather than spreading upgrades across ratepayer bases.

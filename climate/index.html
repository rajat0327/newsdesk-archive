What should state policymakers do about data centers?

This episode analyzes the rapid growth of data center electricity demand, noting that 15 states accounted for 80% of US demand in 2023. A single hyperscale campus can consume 15% to 20% of a utility's capacity, while building the necessary transmission takes 5 to 15 years.

Climate Change Is Rewriting Europe’s Nuclear Cooling Assumptions

Romanian engineers used 180 kg of explosives to clear a rock outcrop in the Danube's Bala Canal. The blast allowed the construction of a temporary dam to maintain cooling water levels for a nuclear plant during low river flows caused by extreme heat.

Why it matters — Highlights how climate-induced low river levels force extreme, invasive civil engineering interventions to prevent thermal power plant shutdowns.

Factcheck: How nuclear, gas, wind and solar power are affected during heatwaves

This analysis examines how extreme heatwaves exceeding 40°C in Europe during June and July 2026 impacted different power sources. The extreme temperatures forced nuclear shutdowns due to cooling limits, reduced gas plant efficiency, dropped wind speeds, and caused electricity networks to sag.

Why it matters — Quantifies the simultaneous derating of multiple generation types during extreme heat, forcing grid operators to plan for correlated capacity drops across nuclear, thermal, and wind.

PJM’s big new data center plan: Make the states figure it out

PJM Interconnection has introduced a plan shifting the responsibility of managing data center load growth onto individual states and utilities. The policy aims to prevent soaring grid upgrade costs from being socialized across all utility customers in its 13-state territory.

Why it matters — Establishes a regulatory precedent where regional transmission organizations refuse to socialize the grid upgrade costs of large digital loads, shifting the financial burden directly to local utilities and states.

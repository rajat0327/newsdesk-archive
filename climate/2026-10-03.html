In global first, a next-gen geothermal plant goes live at utility scale

Fervo Energy has successfully connected its Cape Station next-generation geothermal project in Utah to the grid, delivering power from an initial 33-megawatt unit. This marks the first time a utility-scale, next-generation geothermal facility has gone live and begun commercial operations.

Why it matters — Proves the commercial and operational viability of next-generation geothermal technology at a utility scale (33 MW) on an active grid.

Mesabi Metallics to build a $15B lower-carbon steel mill in Iowa

Mesabi Metallics, owned by India's Essar Group, has announced plans to construct a $15 billion low-carbon steel plant in southeast Iowa. The project aims to utilize ore from the company's nearby iron mine to establish a domestic, lower-emission steelmaking facility.

Why it matters — Represents a massive $15 billion capital commitment to scale low-carbon steel production by co-locating processing with domestic iron ore extraction.

Ameren Missouri Proposes Pollution-Heavy Future to Power Data Centers

Ameren Missouri has filed a long-range energy plan that drops its net-zero emissions goal and increases reliance on fossil fuels. The utility cites the massive surge in electricity demand from new data centers as the primary driver for extending fossil fuel operations.

Why it matters — Illustrates the direct trade-off utilities face when forced to choose between meeting immediate, massive data center load growth and maintaining long-term decarbonization targets.

Reach Stackers Look Like A Hydrogen Use Case. Ports Are Buying Batteries.

Despite heavy duty cycles and weights exceeding 70 tonnes, ports are increasingly choosing battery-electric reach stackers over hydrogen fuel cell alternatives. The post explores the operational trade-offs of using heavy batteries to lift 40-tonne shipping containers in busy terminals.

Analysis: Wind and solar save UK from gas imports worth £5.9bn during Hormuz crisis

The UK has avoided £5.9 billion in gas import costs during the Hormuz energy crisis due to record generation from wind and solar, which grew 14% year-on-year. This clean energy surge helped insulate the grid from soaring global gas prices.

Why it matters — Quantifies the direct economic hedge that domestic wind and solar generation provide against volatile global fossil fuel import prices during geopolitical crises.

Industry & Heat

What’s up with heat-pump water heaters?

Heat pump water heaters use a fraction of the energy of conventional electric resistance heaters, which consume more electricity annually than an EV. However, they make up only 2% of the market because they are expensive, slow, noisy, and require both a plumber and an electrician for installation.

Why it matters — Electric resistance water heaters consume more electricity annually than an EV, but transitioning them to heat pumps is bottlenecked by the installation complexity of requiring both plumbing and electrical trade labor.

Grid & Transmission

DOE Funds 31 Grid Projects, Adds 23 GW

On September 24, 2026, the U.S. Department of Energy announced its intention to provide $1.9 billion in federal funding for 31 grid-improvement projects across 26 states, with a total project value of $5.25 billion. These initiatives are projected to add over 23 GW of electricity capacity by upgrading more than 1,500 miles of transmission lines and deploying Grid-Enhancing Technologies across nearly 21,000 miles.

Why it matters — This investment accelerates grid modernization and expansion by leveraging existing infrastructure, directly increasing transmission capacity and reliability without requiring entirely new corridors.

Data Centre Demand

Data Center Demand Strains US Grids

The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) forecasts record electricity sales in the United States for 2026 (4,135 billion kWh) and 2027 (4,211 billion kWh), primarily driven by data center development and increased manufacturing. Global data center demand is projected to approach 1,050 terawatt-hours (TWh) annually by 2026, which would rank it as the fifth-largest electricity consumer worldwide if it were a country.

Why it matters — The escalating power consumption from data centers and AI is creating an urgent need for utilities to rapidly build new generation and transmission infrastructure, impacting grid stability and electricity costs.
